## Available actions {#actions}

CloakView has global shortcuts for **quick hide** (show or hide the private overlay), **push-to-ask** (show the Ask window, bringing the hub back if it was hidden), **start/stop listen**, and **read screen**. You can use them while another app is focused. These controls do not join or modify your call.

## Configure your keys {#configure}

The defaults are Control–Option–H (quick hide), Command–Shift–Space (push-to-ask), Control–Option–L (start/stop listen) and Command–Shift–S (read screen). Quick hide and listen avoid Command–Shift–H and Command–Shift–L, which Finder and Safari use. Change them in **CloakView → Settings → Shortcuts**. Choose combinations that do not conflict with your meeting app or macOS. Save changes and test each action in a harmless window.

## If a shortcut does nothing {#conflicts}

Try its button in the app first. If another app already holds the combination, the hub shows an error when CloakView starts or when you save your shortcuts; change it in CloakView Settings or the other app. Quick hide and listen use Control–Option so they keep working while macOS secure input is on — for example in Terminal with Secure Keyboard Entry, or while a password field is focused. If you choose a combination with only Option and Shift, it types a character and won't fire in that state; one with Control or Command will. If the shortcut starts listening, check capture and microphone permissions, plus your API key, before testing again. A hidden hub is not proof that capture stopped: use **Stop Listen** to end capture.
